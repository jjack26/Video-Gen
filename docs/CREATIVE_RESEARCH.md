# Clash content research: evidence and production standard

Research checked October 22, 2026, Central time. This is an initial evidence review and a collection protocol, not a completed competitive performance study. No new edit has been approved. No promise of views is supported.

## What the first edit got wrong

The rejected preview combined an interchangeable losing-streak joke, unrelated reaction shots, a uniform center crop and large text. It did not establish a character objective or a causal payoff. The username overlay did not improve the story. This is explicit human feedback, not an inferred performance result. The private test must not become the creative template.

## Platform evidence

### YouTube: watch choice, continued attention and enjoyment

YouTube says Shorts ranking considers whether viewers choose to watch, average view duration, average percentage viewed and satisfaction signals. It does not favor a particular Short format. Competition and topic interest can change distribution even with good metrics. There is no minimum posting cadence required to perform well. Practical inference: test the opening image and the earned payoff, rather than trying to compensate with volume or hashtag lists.

Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en

### Creator practice: immediate visual stakes

A YouTube interview with Jenny Hoyos emphasizes an immediate hook and a compact story. This is an experienced creator's method, not a platform-wide one-second ranking threshold. For this account, test an opening that shows a visible problem: an approaching threat, a character's decision or a consequence already in motion. Do not translate the advice into generic overlay text.

Source: https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/

### Instagram: measure attention rather than headline views

Meta documents total and average watch time for Reels and describes using these to diagnose hooks and slow sections. Total watch time includes replay time. Its examples are general creator advice, not Clash-specific effect estimates. Practical inference: log watch time alongside reach and shares when authorized metrics are available. Do not compare raw play counts to YouTube engaged views without retaining definitions.

Source: https://about.fb.com/news/2023/04/instagram-reels-trending-audio-and-gifts-updates/

### TikTok: useful creative principles, limited transfer

TikTok's Creative Codes discusses structure, early attention, sound and platform-native creative. This material concerns advertising. Its ad recall and watch-time findings cannot be presented as guarantees for organic Clash posts. Use a clear visual opening, purposeful progression and intentional sound as testable craft choices. The accessible search results for TikTok's recommendation explanations also identify viewing and skipping behavior; full direct-page retrieval was unavailable in this session.

Sources: https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/creative-codes and https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/TikTok_CreativeCodes_May2023.pdf

### Originality: a crop and new caption are not a strategy

Meta's Facebook guidance explicitly prioritizes original content and deprioritizes unoriginal work. This source concerns Facebook, not an exact Instagram or TikTok policy. Its relevance is directional: establish creative value with a new narrative, visual construction or substantive perspective. Platform recommendation eligibility and copyright permission are separate checks.

Source: https://about.fb.com/news/2026/03/rewarding-original-creators-on-facebook/

## Clash-specific creative references

### Professional narrative shorts

Roof Studio's first-party case study describes ClashterPieces as three 30-second stories derived from real player moments, with distinct animation styles. It credits specialist storyboard, layout, animation, lighting and sound-related production work. The studio reports millions of viewers across official channels, but provides no audited organic split, per-film retention or comparable small-account baseline. This is a craft reference, not proof that reposting the work will perform.

Inference: build around one specific gameplay event and an emotional turn. Separate concept, shot selection, editing and independent review. Do not recycle the campaign's stories as original ideas.

Source: https://roofstudio.tv/work/clash-royale

### Audience taste is divided

A Reddit discussion contains both nostalgia for older 3D animation and preference for newer 2D styles. It is an older, self-selected qualitative sample. It does not establish what the whole audience wants or which style receives more views. A newer surfaced thread argues the reverse, criticizing newer 3D models. That disagreement is useful counterevidence to any claim that one style always wins.

Sources: https://www.reddit.com/r/ClashRoyale/comments/1eh525w/why_has_clash_royales_youtube_videos_changed/ and https://www.reddit.com/r/ClashRoyale/comments/1w1ro9l/oh_my_days_can_we_stop_with_the_3d_models/

### Rights and asset access

Supercell's fan-content rules govern eligible use of its assets. Official publication is not a copyright waiver. Check footage and music separately. An editorial reference is not automatically a cleared production asset.

Source: https://supercell.com/en/fan-content-policy/

## Access and confidence

YouTube: official guidance and animation catalogue surfaced. Reliable current per-video metrics and comparable creator cohorts have not been collected.

TikTok: official creative materials surfaced. Direct current creator-clip research was unavailable through search in this session. No claim of scanning the feed.

Instagram: Meta's analytics documentation was retrieved. Individual relevant Reels were not reliably retrievable. Indexed popular-topic pages are not accepted as clip evidence.

Reddit: direct audience discussions were retrieved. These are qualitative observations, not representative polling.

X: no clip-level evidence collected. Not connected.

Private account analytics: not collected for this research. No retention or reach data was fabricated.

## Research desk acceptance standard

Before a concept is called research-backed, collect a practical first cohort of 24 direct clips: eight each from YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Instagram Reels, using authorized access where required. Include small and larger accounts, recent and older posts, apparent successes and ordinary performers. This cohort is planned, not completed. Use Reddit and X to find audience language, objections and counterexamples rather than treating votes as equivalent to video views.

For every clip record the exact permalink, creator, publish date, observation timestamp, duration, visible metric names and values, audience scale when visible, first visual action, setup, escalation, payoff, cut timing, framing, sound choices, comments and usage status. Record unavailable metrics as null. Separate clip observations from policy documents. Deduplicate cross-posts of the same creative work.

Compare each candidate to that creator's nearby comparable posts rather than assigning a universal views threshold. A high ratio is an observational lead, not causal proof. Never compare paid brand campaigns directly with a new organic fan account. Flag repeated concepts and identical footage to estimate saturation. Recheck facts against official game sources.

## Production acceptance standard

Each concept must say who wants what, what changes, and why the last shot pays off the first. Explain what is new relative to at least two direct references. Name the exact available shots and any missing material. No text, subtitles, username, logo or decorative filler overlays by default. Do not remove existing third-party attribution to hide provenance.

Editing uses a reviewed decision list: source in/out points, reason for each cut, subject focus, audio treatment and transition purpose. Check character faces, hands and the action target on a real phone-sized preview. A fixed center crop is not an acceptable framing decision for every shot. Preserve deliberate breathing room where the scene needs it; fast cutting is not a substitute for rhythm.

QA must independently watch the export, state whether the story makes sense without explanatory text, inspect audio transitions and clipping, check aspect ratio and full decode, and report timestamped fixes. Technical checks alone cannot mark a video creatively approved. Initial public distribution requires the human to approve the exact preview.

## Experiment plan

Start with three materially different visual stories after the research cohort is available. Test one variable at a time where feasible, such as the opening shot or payoff timing. Keep the final export and creative version linked to its metrics. Compare at consistent 24-hour and seven-day observation windows. Use completion, average watch time, shares per reached viewer and follows per reached viewer when those are actually provided. Tiny samples remain inconclusive. A private upload only checks the publishing route; it does not test public audience demand.
